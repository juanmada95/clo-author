# Frontier Map — TDC in Initial Teacher Training (Spain, Infantil + Primaria)

**Project:** Curricular analysis of how the *Grado en Maestro de Educación Infantil* and *Grado en Maestro de Educación Primaria* in Spain develop Teacher Digital Competence (TDC), benchmarked against **DigCompEdu** (Redecker & Punie, 2017) and the **MRCDD** (BOE-A-2022-8042).
**Companion file:** `annotated_bibliography.md` (51 entries, 7 buckets).
**Date:** 2026-04-26.

**Verdict on Bucket A:** the field has approached the project's question from **three different sides** — by subject subset (EFL: A1, A3), by competence subset (inclusion: A4), and by curricular layer (*planes de estudio*: A5–A6) — but **no published study has done a comprehensive, full-degree, both-degrees, MRCDD-benchmarked curricular analysis using both the *memoria de verificación* layer and the *guía docente* layer**. The Norwegian methodological precedent (A7, A8) is exportable and underpins the design.

**Verdict on Bucket B:** **saturated.** This bucket is the dominant publication mode in Spanish-language TDC research. It establishes the "demand side" baseline (what trainee teachers report) but does not address the "supply side" question (what the curriculum delivers). It is useful as **context** in the introduction and as a **comparison anchor** in the discussion: the project's curricular findings can be juxtaposed against documented self-perceived TDC levels (~B1 across studies).

### Bucket E — Foundational and theoretical references

The SQD model (E1, E2, E3, E4) is the dominant pedagogical framework for thinking about *how* ITE prepares teachers for technology integration. Goodlad's curriculum-levels framework (E6) is the analytical scaffold transferred from A7 — **adopt this for the project** to distinguish ideological (BOE/LOMLOE), formal (*memoria de verificación*), perceived (*guía docente*), and operational (classroom practice) curricula. Esteve-Mon, Castañeda & Adell (2018, E5) provides the Spanish-language holistic TDC model.

## 2. The gap statement

> Across the Spanish initial-teacher-training literature, the curricular *supply* of teacher digital competence in the *Grado en Maestro de Educación Infantil* and *Grado en Maestro de Educación Primaria* has been studied only **partially** — restricted by subject area (EFL — A1, A3), by competence subset (digital inclusion — A4), or by curricular layer (only *planes de estudio*, predating the MRCDD — A5, A6). **No published study comprehensively maps the full DigCompEdu / MRCDD competence space across both degrees, at both the *memoria de verificación* and *guía docente* levels, across the Spanish university system after the MRCDD entered force.** The Norwegian curricular-analysis precedents (A7, A8) demonstrate that this design is feasible and methodologically defensible.

## 3. The white space — where the project lives

| Dimension | Existing literature | Project's position |
|---|---|---|
| Geography | Spain (mostly autonomic samples) or Norway | **All-Spain coverage** (or a defensible subset) |
| Degrees | Primary alone (A1, A3); both at *plan* level (A5, A6) | **Both Infantil and Primaria** at full curricular detail |
| Subject scope | Subject subsets (EFL — A1, A3) or competence subsets (inclusion — A4) | **Entire degree** competences, both subject-specific and transversal |
| Curricular layer | *Plan de estudios* (A5, A6) or *guía docente* alone (A1, A3) | **Two layers: *memoria de verificación* + *guía docente*** (Goodlad's formal vs. perceived) |
| Reference framework | TPACK (A3); DigCompEdu (A1, A4); pre-MRCDD INTEF (A5, A6) | **MRCDD as coding grid + DigCompEdu as European benchmark** |
| Temporal moment | Pre-MRCDD (A5, A6) or partial post-MRCDD (A1, A3, A4) | **Post-MRCDD (post-2022)** — first comprehensive snapshot |

## 4. Risks flagged for the strategist

1. **Scooping risk — Cuevas-Monzonís cluster (A1, A3).** Same data corpus (39 universities × *guías docentes*), same Primary degree, but restricted to EFL. The project must defend why a **whole-degree, two-degree, two-layer** analysis is sufficiently distinct (it is — but the framing must be deliberate).
2. **Scooping risk — Sanz-Benito, Lázaro-Cantabrana & Grimalt-Álvaro (A4).** Same authors as the central Lázaro-Cantabrana / COMDID cluster; same target population. Same defense applies — **scope restricted to inclusion**, the project covers all 6 areas.
3. **Coverage feasibility.** "All Spanish universities" is the strongest framing; if infeasible, a defensible subset (public universities; one autonomous community + a comparison region; the largest providers) must be justified.
4. **Layer collapse.** If *memorias de verificación* and *guías docentes* turn out to be largely identical at the digital-competence level, the two-layer design loses its bite. Pilot 3–5 universities before committing.
5. **MRCDD vs. DigCompEdu coding decision.** Either can serve as the primary grid; collapsing into a hybrid coding scheme risks intercoder confusion. The strategist should commit to one as primary.
